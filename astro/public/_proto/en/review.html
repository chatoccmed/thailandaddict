The full review

If you've ever seen those photos of Railay's towering limestone cliffs and white-sand beaches and wished you could actually stay right there — Rayavadee is the place sitting in the middle of that picture. This 5-star luxury resort occupies the very tip of the Railay headland, wrapped by beaches on three sides (Railay West, Railay East and Phra Nang), and it's reachable by longtail boat only.

What sets Rayavadee apart from other resorts in Krabi is its location. It occupies the very tip of the Railay headland, wrapped by white-sand beaches on three sides — Railay West, Railay East and Phra Nang — with towering limestone cliffs as a backdrop. The headland has no road access; cars can't reach it, so the only way in is by longtail boat from Ao Nang Pier. It's precisely this hard-to-reach quality that keeps the atmosphere quiet and makes you feel cut off from the outside world — the reason so many reviews call a stay here a once-in-a-lifetime experience.

Rooms are two-storey Thai-style pavilions scattered through lush tropical gardens. They start with the Deluxe Pavilion for two, moving up to the very spacious Terrace Pavilion at around 115 sqm. For maximum privacy there's the Pool Pavilion with a private plunge pool, and the 167 sqm Family Pavilion with Pool that sleeps four, ideal for families. The design leans into Thai character mixed with luxury, all set amid nature. Most reviews praise the generous space and the calm, though some note that a few pavilions are starting to show their age.

"You take a longtail boat in, and the moment you step onto the sand and see the cliffs and sea all around, it feels like another world — worth every baht we saved up for."

On the beaches and pool. Rayavadee gives you direct access to some of Thailand's most famous beaches — step out of the resort and you're at Phra Nang, Railay West and Railay East. The resort's own pool is beautiful and blends into the surrounding gardens. All around there's nature and wildlife to spot — birds, monkeys and big old trees. This is the atmosphere so many people come to Railay for, and here you get it within walking distance of your room.

The dining highlight is The Grotto — a restaurant set inside a natural limestone cave right by Phra Nang beach. Having dinner in a cave with the sea in front of you is one of the images reviews mention most often. Beyond that, the resort has several restaurants and a breakfast many guests rate highly. Service overall is another thing guests bring up again and again — staff are warm, attentive and genuinely friendly, not just polite for form's sake.

Now for the things worth being straight about before you book. The first and biggest is price — Rayavadee is one of the most expensive resorts in Krabi, starting in the mid-teens of thousands per night, with villas far higher. The second is that food and drink prices inside the resort run high, enough that some reviews suggest walking out to cheaper restaurants in Railay for some meals. These two points are worth budgeting for.

Another thing to know concerns getting there and privacy. Because it's reachable by boat only, hauling your luggage on and off the boat can get wet or awkward, especially at high or low tide or when the sea is rough — go in prepared for that. And although the resort itself is private, the beaches around it are public, and day-trippers come in numbers during the middle of the day, especially at Phra Nang and Railay West. If you want an empty beach, head out early morning or in the evening when it's quieter.

Another thing reviews warn about often is the monkeys. There are monkeys living around the resort, and some are fairly bold, prone to snatching food and belongings. Don't leave food or valuables out on your balcony or by the beach, and shut your doors and windows properly when you leave the room. It's just the nature of a spot tucked into forested cliffs by the sea — not a fault of the resort itself, but good to know so you can be careful.

In short, Rayavadee suits people who are set on coming to Railay for a special, high-end stay — a honeymoon, an anniversary, or a trip where you'll pay for top-tier location and service. Its 9.4 on Booking (9.6 from couples) and #1 ranking in Railay on TripAdvisor reflect that most guests come away genuinely impressed. But if your budget is tight, or you can't accept getting there by boat and the resort's food prices, Railay still has plenty of more affordable places to choose from in the same area.

Tips before you go

Allow time for the boat and luggage

It's reachable by longtail boat from Ao Nang Pier only · at low tide or in rough seas you may have to wade a little, so pack a waterproof bag and shoes that slip off easily.

Walk the beach at dawn and dusk

Phra Nang and Railay West get plenty of day-trippers during the middle of the day · head out at first light or near sunset for an emptier beach and prettier light.

Watch out for snatching monkeys

There are monkeys around the resort, and some are bold · don't leave food or valuables out on the balcony or by the beach, and shut doors and windows properly when you leave the room.

Eat at Railay restaurants sometimes

Food and drink inside the resort are pricey · walking out to restaurants in Railay for some meals is far cheaper and gives you a different atmosphere.

Frequently asked questions — Rayavadee Krabi

Where is Rayavadee and how do I get there?

It's on the Railay headland-Phra Nang beach in Mueang Krabi district · the headland has no road access, so cars can't reach it · it's reachable by longtail boat from Ao Nang Pier only, about 15 minutes by boat · from Krabi Airport it's roughly 30–40 minutes by car to the pier, then the boat.

Why is Rayavadee reachable only by boat?

Because the resort sits on the Railay headland, wrapped by limestone cliffs and the sea on three sides, with no connecting road · the only way is by longtail boat · this hard-to-reach quality keeps the atmosphere quiet and private, but it also means hauling luggage on and off the boat can be awkward and may get wet at high or low tide.

How much does Rayavadee cost and what rooms are there?

From around ฿13,900/night for a Deluxe Pavilion (2 people) · Terrace Pavilion (~115 sqm) around ฿16,500 · Pool Pavilion with a private pool around ฿23,500 · Family Pavilion with Pool (167 sqm, 4 people) around ฿32,600 · it's one of the highest-priced resorts in Krabi, prices move with the season, so compare across platforms before booking.

Who is Rayavadee best for?

It's ideal for people set on coming to Railay for a special luxury stay — a honeymoon, an anniversary, or a trip where you'll pay for top-tier location and service · couples score it as high as 9.6 on Booking · it's less suited to those on a tight budget, or anyone who can't accept getting there by boat and the resort's fairly high food prices.

What is The Grotto?

The Grotto is Rayavadee's restaurant set inside a natural limestone cave by Phra Nang beach · it's the highlight reviews mention most often, because you get to eat a meal in a cave with the sea in front of you · the resort also has other restaurants and a breakfast many guests rate highly.

When is the best time to visit Railay-Krabi?

The high season, November–March, has clear seas and good weather, best for swimming, rock climbing and beach days · the rainy season (May–October) is cheaper and less crowded, but the sea can be choppy and boat travel may be affected, so check the forecast before you go.
