In short: Talat Phlu–Wongwian Yai is on the Thonburi side, with BTS Wongwian Yai and Talat Phlu on the Silom line running straight into Sathorn and Siam. The draw is Talat Phlu, an old street-food district, and stays here lean toward three-star hotels and value guesthouses a few minutes' walk from the stations. The Chao Phraya is close by, and rooms start around ฿700 a night. It's a good base for budget travellers who want the train and real food on the doorstep.

Things to do in Talat Phlu

Highlights and spots visitors love in this area

Talat Phlu Market

A historic Thonburi market legendary for street food across generations, busiest from late afternoon into the night for grazing

Wongwian Yai & King Taksin Monument

A large traffic circle anchoring Thonburi, centred on the equestrian monument to King Taksin the Great, a defining local landmark

Wongwian Yai Railway Station

Terminus of the Maeklong Railway's Maha Chai line, where slow local trains rumble out past gardens toward Samut Sakhon

Princess Mother Memorial Park

A leafy riverside park on the Princess Mother's childhood home site, with a museum recreating her early life and times

Kudi Chin & Santa Cruz Church

A riverside Portuguese-descended community anchored by the red-domed Santa Cruz Church, famous for its heritage Kudi Chin cake

Kanom Farang Kudi Chin

A 300-year-old Portuguese-style cake topped with raisins and candied gourd, charcoal-baked and found only in Kudi Chin

Wat Prayoon

A riverside temple with a UNESCO-awarded great white chedi and an artificial hill ringed by a turtle-filled pond

Wat Kalayanamit

A riverside temple near Pak Khlong housing Luang Pho To, a giant seated Buddha, and reputedly Thailand's largest bell

Pak Khlong Talat Flower Market

Bangkok's largest flower market, just across the river by bridge, alive day and night with the scent of fresh blooms

Wat Intharam

A Thonburi-era temple enshrining King Taksin's remains, with fine murals and architecture, a short walk from Talat Phlu

Wat Sai area & canal life

Nearby Bang Luang and Bangkok Yai canals, where boat rides reveal old Thonburi waterside houses and orchard life

Where to eat in Talat Phlu

The dishes and spots this area is known for

Jeen Lee crispy rice noodles

Crispy rice noodles from a recipe over 130 years old, four generations on, sweet-sour glazed, a Talat Phlu must-try

Talat Phlu red pork noodles

Chewy noodles with roasted red pork or shrimp dumplings in clear broth, long-running stalls with queues from late afternoon

BBQ pork over rice

Tender BBQ pork and crispy pork in sweet-savoury sauce over hot rice with egg, from a stall running over half a century

Talat Phlu chive dumplings

Thin-skinned, generously stuffed chive cakes griddled on charcoal until fragrant, dipped in sweet soy, a long-made Teochew recipe

Muay Lee frozen egg ice cream

Coconut ice cream blended with egg yolk, topped with corn, red bean and crispy rubies, a sweet-savoury Talat Phlu classic

Talat Phlu Thai sweets

Foi thong, thong yip, thong yot and layered khanom chan from grandmothers' recipes, prettily coloured and found all over

Pa thong ko & soy milk

Fried dough sticks, crisp outside and soft within, paired with hot soy milk, a beloved morning and evening snack here

Khanom bueang Thai crisp crepes

Thin crisp folded crepes filled with sweet golden threads or creamed coconut, made fresh to order around Talat Phlu

Oyster omelette & pad thai

Crisp oyster omelette and fresh-shrimp pad thai fired in hot woks, a popular evening street-food pick at several stalls
