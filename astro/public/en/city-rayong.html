Things to do in Rayong

Highlights and sights around Rayong — nature, city and culture

Koh Samet

The island off Ban Phe, with clear water and white sand a short boat ride away. There's Sai Kaew Beach, Ao Phrao, and smaller coves to choose from for a stay

Mae Ramphueng Beach

A long sandy beach in Rayong town, with a beachfront road, seafood restaurants, and places to stay lined up along the water for a relaxed sit

Ban Phe

The pier for Koh Samet and the province's big souvenir market, selling shrimp paste, fish sauce, dried squid, and all sorts of dried seafood

Yom Chinda Road

An old town along the Rayong River, with old wooden buildings, nobles' houses, coffee shops, and local snacks to wander past

Pak Nam Prasae

An old fishing community at the mouth of the Prasae River, with HTMS Prasae moored as a memorial, the Prasae Sin Bridge, and the golden Prong mangrove field

Fruit orchards

Rayong is a durian, rambutan, and mangosteen town. In summer the orchards open up for all-you-can-eat fruit and to buy some to take home

Where to eat in Rayong

Rayong's signature food — real local spots, rounded up and ranked

Beachfront seafood

Mae Ramphueng Beach, Ban Phe, and the coast are lined with seafood spots serving fresh shrimp, shellfish, crab, and squid at reasonable prices

Dried seafood and shrimp paste & fish sauce

Ban Phe sells dried squid, dried shrimp, shrimp paste, and Rayong's well-known fish sauce. People stop to buy them as gifts before heading back

Rayong noodles and khanom chin

In town there are long-running noodle shops and khanom chin with crab nam ya that locals have eaten for ages

Durian, rambutan, mangosteen

In summer Rayong's orchard fruit comes in fully. Mon Thong durian and Rong Rian rambutan are easy to find at stalls and orchards

Sweets and local snacks

Rayong has kalamae, khao lam, and folk sweets sold at the Ban Phe market and souvenir shops

Cafes in town and by the sea

The Yom Chinda area and Mae Ramphueng beachfront have cafes in old buildings and coffee shops with sea views to stop and rest

Mu kratha and street food

Around the residential areas and worker dorms near the industrial estate there are mu kratha (DIY hotpot-grill) spots and street food for an evening meal

About — Rayong

Rayong sits east of Chonburi, about a 2–3 hour drive from Bangkok. It has the clear water of Koh Samet, the long and quiet Mae Ramphueng Beach, and orchards that ripen durian, rambutan, and mangosteen over the summer.

People like to drive down for the seafood, pick up shrimp paste and fish sauce to take home, then catch a boat out to Koh Samet to laze on the sand. If you want an easy trip, wander the Yom Chinda old town, try some noodles, stop at a cafe, and carry on to the coast at Ban Phe or the river mouth at Pak Nam Prasae.

Best time: November–February, when the sea is clear and calm and good for island trips; April–June is orchard fruit season
