Things to do in Pattaya

Highlights and sights around Pattaya — nature, city and culture

Jomtien Beach

A long sandy beach south of central Pattaya, quieter and more family-friendly than the city-center beach. Plenty of beachfront restaurants, water sports, and seaside hotels.

Sanctuary of Truth

An entirely hand-carved wooden temple on the waterfront in Naklua, with intricate woodwork on every surface. A landmark for photos and a look into Eastern philosophy.

Nong Nooch Garden Pattaya

A large botanical garden with themed sections — a French garden, a dinosaur valley, plus elephant and Thai cultural shows. Easily a full day of wandering.

Khao Chi Chan

A giant Buddha image laser-carved into a large rock cliff, a place to pay respects and take photos that you can spot from far off. It sits along the Pattaya–Sattahip road.

Big Buddha on Pratumnak Hill

A large Buddha image on Pratumnak Hill in central Pattaya. Head up to pay respects and take in the wide view over Pattaya Bay, near the Pratumnak viewpoint.

Koh Larn

A clear-water island just off Pattaya — take a boat over to swim and snorkel for the day. Ta Waen, Samae, and Nual beaches have white sand and clear water, and it's Pattaya's most popular sea trip.

Where to eat in Pattaya

Pattaya's signature food — real local spots, rounded up and ranked

Naklua seafood

The Naklua area has markets and seafood restaurants with fresh catch at fair prices — prawns, shellfish, crab, and fish cooked fresh by the sea. This is where Pattaya locals actually go for seafood.

Buffets and beachfront restaurants

Pattaya has plenty of seafood buffets, grills, and beachfront spots along Jomtien. Sit down to eat while the sun sets over Pattaya Bay.

Rooftop bars and cafes

The high-rises along the beach have rooftop bars with sea views, and fresh-coffee cafes are scattered across Jomtien, Pratumnak, and the old town — chill spots that photograph well.

Thepprasit Night Market

Thepprasit Night Market brings together street food, snacks, and shopping at friendly prices. It's a go-to evening stroll for locals and tourists alike.

Korean and Japanese food

With its mix of nationalities, Pattaya has serious Korean and Japanese food districts — barbecue, ramen, and good sushi at several spots, especially around Central and Jomtien.

Local Thai food and street eats

Beyond the tourist restaurants, Pattaya still has made-to-order shops, noodle stalls, and rice-and-curry joints at local prices tucked down the side streets — bold flavors and good value.

About — Pattaya

Pattaya is a seaside resort town in Chonburi province, only about two hours from Bangkok, which makes it an easy-in, easy-out destination for Thais and foreign visitors alike. It has a bit of everything: long sandy beaches, theme parks, shows, big malls, and one of the liveliest nightlife scenes in the country.

You can pick your zone in Pattaya. Central Pattaya Beach is busy and right next to the malls and Walking Street; Jomtien Beach is longer and quieter, better for families; and Naklua–Wong Amat is a calmer stretch by the sea. Top sights include the all-wood Sanctuary of Truth on the waterfront, the beautifully landscaped Nong Nooch Garden, the Buddha image carved into the cliff at Khao Chi Chan, and the Big Buddha on Pratumnak Hill. If you want clear water, you can take a boat over to Koh Larn for the day. · Honesty: Pattaya has a clearly defined nightlife district — go out sensibly and keep an eye on your valuables.

Best time: November–February is high season, with cool, comfortable weather and clear water · March–May is very hot · The rainy season runs June–October with on-and-off showers, but indoor spots, malls, and shows are fine year-round
