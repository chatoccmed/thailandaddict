Things to do in Maha Sarakham

Highlights and sights around Maha Sarakham — nature, city and culture

Phra That Na Dun

The Buddhist center of Isan in Na Dun district, where Buddha relics and Dvaravati-era artifacts were found. It's the spiritual heart of Maha Sarakham.

Mahasarakham University

This is a student town. The area around the university is packed with dorms, restaurants, and cafes that stay open late.

Ku Santarat and the ancient ruins

A Khmer-era stone sanctuary in Na Dun district that once served as an arogayasala, an old hospital — a clear trace of Khmer civilization.

Kaeng Loeng Chan

A reservoir near town where locals come to catch the breeze, cycle, and grab a riverside bite in the evening.

Ban Mo pottery village

An old pottery village in Mueang district where people still throw clay pots and household ware by hand the traditional way.

Maha Sarakham's museums and antiquities

The town holds plenty of Dvaravati- and Khmer-era artifacts. Track down the carved stone boundary markers and terracotta votive tablets dug up around the area.

Where to eat in Maha Sarakham

Maha Sarakham's signature food — real local spots, rounded up and ranked

Isan food

Som tam, larb, koi, grilled chicken eaten with sticky rice — found on every corner, bold and fiery the way real Isan food is.

Food around the university

The Tha Khon Yang area and the streets around MSU are full of rice-and-curry stalls, made-to-order shops, and student-priced snacks open till late.

Mu kratha and jim jum

DIY grill-and-hotpot joints are all over town — a go-to dinner for students and workers alike.

Isan sausage and mam

Tangy fermented sausages grilled and eaten with fresh vegetables and sticky rice — both a snack and a classic local souvenir.

Cafes and coffee

Being a student town means lots of cafes around the university and in the center — good for working or reading all day.

Noodles and bites by Kaeng Loeng Chan

Restaurants and snack stalls line the reservoir, easy to settle in for a meal in the cool evening breeze.

Pla ra and local ferments

Pla ra, jaew bong, and other punchy ferments are staples of the Isan kitchen — pick them up at the town's fresh markets.

Morning markets and night markets

The town markets have breakfast bites from sticky rice with grilled pork to khanom jeen, plus grilled snacks to graze on at night.

About — Maha Sarakham

Maha Sarakham is an education town in the heart of Isan. Two big universities fill the place with students, cafes, and budget-friendly restaurants. Around town you'll find Phra That Na Dun, considered the Buddhist center of the northeast, scattered Khmer-era ruins across several districts, and craft villages where people still do real basketry and weaving. If you want small-town Isan that's easy to get around, where food is cheap and there's history to track down, Maha Sarakham is worth a stop.

Best time: November–February, cool and comfortable — good for walking the town and tracking down the old Khmer ruins
